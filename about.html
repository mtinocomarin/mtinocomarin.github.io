About me / Bay Area engineer

Engineer, Researcher & Mentor

I'm a Bay Area engineer and graduate researcher at SFSU. I build embedded and robotic systems, teach engineering labs, and mentor undergraduate researchers.

My path

How I got here.

I grew up in the Bay Area and earned my bachelor's degree in Computer Engineering at San Francisco State University. I first got involved in research as an undergraduate, where I learned how to turn an idea into working hardware by writing low-level firmware, debugging circuits, and testing prototypes at the bench.

I'm now pursuing my master's degree in Electrical and Computer Engineering at SFSU. My current work brings embedded control together with robotics, machine learning, computer vision, and biosignals. I'm especially interested in real-time systems that can make assistive technology more responsive and intuitive.

I also enjoy building the software behind the experiments. That includes Python tools for hardware communication, recording, visualization, data analysis, and file management. These tools may not be the most visible part of a project, but they make the research easier to run and repeat.

Outside of my own research, I teach engineering labs and help mentor two undergraduate researchers. I enjoy explaining difficult ideas, working through problems with students, and watching them become more confident engineers.

I want to keep working on challenging embedded and robotic systems with teams that care about both strong engineering and real-world usefulness.
